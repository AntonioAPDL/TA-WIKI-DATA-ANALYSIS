# TA Wiki descriptive-survey writing profile

Article type: structured-only departmental descriptive survey/evaluation.

Primary summaries: use counts and item-specific valid-response denominators.
Use percentages only when paired with `n/N`, and avoid decimal precision in
main-text interpretation.

Evidence boundaries:

- Do not infer response rate, department-wide prevalence, representativeness,
  causality, intervention effectiveness, respondent-level routing, or
  unique-respondent status without verified evidence.
- Treat conditional items as non-primary unless respondent-level parent-item
  routing has been verified.
- Treat the direct contribution item as primary. The principal sensitivity is
  the extreme-case deterministic missing-response range; the reason-informed
  diagnostic is secondary, routing-dependent, and internal unless routing is
  verified.
- The desired-features item describes a general departmental teaching resource,
  not necessarily the TA Wiki. Present Wiki changes as candidate pilots.
- Exclude qualitative themes, quotations, and paraphrases until manual coding,
  independent review/adjudication, snapshotting, and disclosure review are
  complete.
- Keep sparse exploratory cross-tabs as internal diagnostics, not manuscript
  evidence.

Writing style:

- Use precise, restrained, evidence-bounded prose.
- Use `survey records` for substantive empirical claims until uniqueness is
  verified.
- Separate observed survey-record reports from interpretation and local
  implementation suggestions.
- Present implementation changes as pilots or hypotheses to be tested.
- Keep audit instructions, unresolved factual requests, and process notes out
  of rendered manuscript prose; place them in coauthor documentation instead.
- Keep main tables compact, readable, and nonduplicative; avoid `\scriptsize`
  in the manuscript PDF.

Do not invent citations, field dates, invitation denominators, ethics wording,
survey routing, or empirical results not generated by the analysis pipeline.
