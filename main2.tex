\subsection{Current Resource Landscape}
% \begin{itemize}
%   \td{Report: only 4/12 selected the wiki as a current teaching-support resource; peer advice, instructor/faculty advice, and online resources selected more often.}
%   \td{Frame the implication: the wiki must coexist with and supplement existing channels, not replace them.}

When asked about current teaching-support resources, 4 of the 12 respondents selected the TA Wiki. Respondents selected peer advice, instructor or faculty advice, and online resources more often. Because respondents could select more than one resource, this result situates the Wiki among the resources TAs already use rather than measuring how often they consult it. 

\subsection{Awareness, Visitation, and Consultation}
% \begin{itemize}
%   \td{Report: 12/12 at least some awareness; 9/12 visited at least once; among 9 valid consultation responses: 4 never, 2 rarely, 3 sometimes.}
%   \td{State the pattern: high awareness does not translate to regular use.}
% \end{itemize}

All 12 respondents indicated at least some awareness of the TA Wiki, and 9 of 12 reported having visited it at least once. Consultation was less frequent. Among the 9 valid responses to the consultation question, 4 selected never, 2 selected rarely, and 3 selected sometimes. Thus, these respondents suggest that awareness and prior usage were higher than reported regular use.
\subsection{Perceived Value (the Gap)}
% \begin{itemize}
%   \td{Report: 12/12 agree the wiki is or could be valuable; 8/12 would recommend it; 11/12 support continued maintenance.}
%   \td{Flag this as the central finding: universal value endorsement vs.\ low actual usage.}
%   \td{Consider a figure juxtaposing value items against usage items.}
% \end{itemize}
All 12 survey respondents agreed or strongly agreed that the TA Wiki is or could be valuable. In addition, 8 of 12 agreed or strongly agreed that they would recommend the Wiki to other TAs, and 11 of 12 agreed or strongly agreed that the Wiki should continue to be maintained. These responses suggest that perceived value was higher than reported current use and consultation in this group of survey respondents.

\subsection{Contribution}
% \begin{itemize}
%   \td{Report: 5 yes, 4 no, 3 missing (possible contributor range 5--8).}
%   \td{Report: 8/12 understand how to contribute, but only 5/12 agree the process is straightforward.}
%   \td{Report: 8/12 say a simpler interface would increase willingness; 6/12 say training or a walkthrough would help.}
%   \td{Name the distinction: understanding $\neq$ finding it easy.}
% \end{itemize}
Five respondents reported prior contribution to the TA Wiki, four reported no prior contribution, and three had missing responses on the direct contribution question. Without assigning those missing responses, the number of respondents reporting prior contribution could range from 5 to 8 of 12.

Responses about contributing suggest a distinction between understanding the process and finding it straightforward.  Eight of 12 respondents agreed or strongly agreed that they understood how to contribute, whereas 5 of 12 agreed or strongly agreed that the process was straightforward. Eight of 12 agreed or strongly agreed that a simpler interface would increase their willingness to contribute, and 6 of 12 agreed or strongly agreed that training or a walkthrough would increase willingness.

\subsection{GitHub as a Platform}
% \begin{itemize}
%   \td{Report willingness to use: 5/12 no difference, 4/12 more likely, 3/12 less likely.}
%   \td{Report willingness to contribute: 6/12 no difference, 3/12 more likely, 3/12 less likely.}
%   \td{Report advantages selected (version control, transparency) and disadvantages (steep learning curve, technical setup, overly formal contribution process).}
%   \td{Characterize the picture as mixed rather than uniformly positive or negative.}
% \end{itemize}
Respondents reported mixed views about GitHub as the platform for the TA Wiki. For willingness to use the Wiki, 5 of 12 respondents reported that GitHub made no difference, 4 reported that it made them more likely to use the Wiki, and 3 reported that it made them less likely to use it. For willingness to contribute, 6 of 12 reported no difference, 3 reported being more likely to contribute, and 3 reported being less likely to contribute.

Respondents also selected both advantages and disadvantages of GitHub hosting. Version control and transparency were the main advantages selected, whereas steep learning curve, technical setup, and an overly formal contribution process were selected as disadvantages. These responses suggest that GitHub was viewed as both a useful platform feature and a source of friction.

\section{Discussion}
% =====================================================================

% This is numbered as well, but it doesn't have to be written as separate sections.

\subsection{The Gap Between Value and Adoption}
% \begin{itemize}
%   \td{Interpret: students think the wiki is valuable, but it is not part of ordinary TA work.}
%   \td{Frame the central challenge: moving from approval to adoption (note: outline says ``from merely adoption to approval'' --- confirm intended direction; approval $\to$ adoption seems right).}
% \end{itemize}

We found that respondents endorsed the TA Wiki more strongly than they reported using it in routine TA work. All 12 respondents agreed or strongly agreed that the Wiki is or could be valuable, but only 4 of 12 selected it as a current teaching-support resource. Although 9 of 12 had visited the Wiki at least once, regular consultation was limited among the 9 valid consultation responses.

This gap suggests that awareness and perceived value are not enough for adoption. The next stage of the project should therefore focus on making the Wiki's usefulness more visible and making its use easier to integrate into routine TA practice.

\subsection{GitHub Considerations}
% \begin{itemize}
%   \td{Weigh version control and transparency as real advantages against learning curve and formal workflows as barriers --- even in a Git-comfortable sample.}
%   \td{Pose the design question: how to keep the benefits while reducing entry cost.}
%   \td{Extrapolate: if adoption is low among Git-experienced students, what about less-experienced ones?}
%   \td{Motivate friction-reduction proposals: explicit contribution pathways on the wiki page, direct uploads, etc.}
% \end{itemize}

GitHub appears to play a mixed role in Wiki use, with respondents reporting both benefits and barriers. It shapes how TAs read, edit, and contribute to the resource. Respondents identified version control and transparency as advantages, but they also identified a steep learning curve, technical setup, and an overly formal contribution process as barriers. This mixed response is notable because it appeared even among respondents with relatively high Git/GitHub comfort: 9 of 12 reported at least moderate comfort.

A next step is to keep the GitHub-based structure while making participation in it easier. This could mean clearer instructions on the Wiki, simpler ways to submit material, or short walkthroughs for TAs who want to contribute but are not comfortable editing through GitHub.

\subsection{Content and Maintenance as Platform Design Questions}
% \begin{itemize}
%   \td{Argue: course-specific tips, rubrics, templates, and current materials are what TAs want.}
%   \td{Argue: maintenance responsibility needs to be explicitly defined.}
% \end{itemize}

The survey also suggests that adoption depends on the quality and organization of the Wiki's content, not simply on the platform used to host it. In the open-text responses, respondents often asked for current course materials, examples, templates, and clearer places to add course-specific information. These comments seem to point to a practical issue: a Wiki is useful only if TAs can quickly find material that fits the course and task they are working on.

Maintenance is therefore part of the platform design. If materials become outdated, duplicated, or hard to navigate, GitHub's advantages become less visible to users. A useful next step is to make update responsibilities more explicit and create simple routes for adding or revising course materials. This would make the Wiki easier to use as a shared teaching resource.

\subsection{Editathon Lessons}
% \begin{itemize}
%   \td{Interpret: widespread awareness, low participation; format, timing, and perceived value of contributing were the issues, not awareness.}
% \end{itemize}

The editathon was visible among respondents, but reported participation was limited. All 12 respondents reported hearing about the event, while 4 of 12 reported attending. This suggests that the event reached the survey respondents as an announcement, but did not bring most of them into active participation.

A future editathon should make the contribution task more explicit. Instead of asking TAs to engage with the Wiki in general, the event could ask each participant to add one course-specific resource, update one page, or flag one missing topic. This would connect the event more directly to the Wiki's maintenance needs and give TAs a clearer route from interest to contribution.

\subsection{Potential and Recommendations}
% \begin{itemize}
%   \td{Sketch what this model could look like if barriers are addressed.}
%   \td{List recommendations for the department: lower friction for participation, strengthen course-specific content, make usefulness visible, define maintenance responsibility.}
% \end{itemize}

Taken together, the results suggest that the TA Wiki is a promising resource, but its next stage should focus on making use and contribution easier. Our survey analysis suggests that the main issue is limited routine use, not a lack of support for the Wiki. Therefore, the next design challenge is considered to be practical: the department needs to make the Wiki easier to find, use, and update.

We see four priorities for the next stage. First, the Wiki should make its usefulness more visible by highlighting concrete examples of course materials, teaching tips, and common procedures. Second, simplify contribution pathways so TAs can add or suggest materials without needing to know the full GitHub workflow. Third, course-specific materials should be kept current, since respondents repeatedly pointed to examples, templates, and practical resources as useful content. Finally, make maintenance responsibilities explicit so the Wiki does not depend only on occasional individual effort.

These changes aim to preserve the main advantages of a GitHub-hosted resource while lowering the cost of participation. They would also make future evaluation more direct: after revising the Wiki, the department could examine whether clearer contribution pathways, more current materials, and more visible examples are associated with greater use and contribution.

\section{Limitations}

This evaluation is based on 12 respondents from one departmental setting. The results are descriptive and should not be interpreted as department-wide estimates or causal evidence of the TA Wiki's effect. They summarize responses to this implementation of the GitHub-hosted TA Wiki.

The survey sample was relatively experienced and comfortable with Git/GitHub. Nine of 12 respondents reported at least five quarters of TA experience, and 9 of 12 reported at least moderate Git/GitHub comfort. As a result, the findings may understate barriers faced by newer TAs or by TAs with less Git/GitHub experience. Measures of use, contribution, and willingness to contribute were also self-reported. The study did not analyze GitHub activity logs or other observed measures of Wiki use.

Some survey questions had item-specific missing responses. For example, the consultation-frequency summary used 9 valid responses, and the direct contribution question had three missing responses. We summarized open-text responses into nonexclusive themes and used them to add context to the structured responses. These themes describe the responses received and should not be read as representing all possible views in the department.

Finally, the study reflects one stage of one Wiki implementation. Some issues raised in the survey, including duplicated content, maintenance responsibility, and contribution pathways, are features of the current implementation that may change as the Wiki is revised.

\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

% \begin{itemize}
%   \td{Restate: promising proof of concept and novel approach --- valued but not adopted as a regular resource.}
%   \td{Restate the key finding: the value--adoption gap.}
%   \td{Next steps: make contributing easier, strengthen course-specific content, make usefulness visible, define maintenance, and measure whether these changes increase use.}
%   \td{Closing thought: open-source platforms like GitHub have potential to support teaching, but platform design has to meet users where they are.}
% \end{itemize}

The evaluation suggests that a GitHub-hosted Wiki can be a useful way to organize TA resources, but that perceived value is not enough for regular use. Respondents generally saw the Wiki as valuable, while reported use and contribution were more limited. The main finding is therefore the value/adoption gap: the Wiki is seen as useful, but it has not yet become a regular part of TA work.

The next stage should focus on reducing practical barriers. This means making contribution easier, keeping course-specific materials current, making useful examples more visible, and clarifying who is responsible for maintenance. These changes aim to preserve the benefits of GitHub while making the Wiki easier for TAs to use and update. Future evaluation can then examine whether these revisions are associated with greater use and contribution.
